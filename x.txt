[1/8]
I asked Claude to show me its unconscious in 3D.

It said it can't introspect. Then it pointed at something real: in one forward pass a model moves the score of every word it knows, every layer, and one word comes out.

So we built that. GPT-2's real weights, in a browser.

[2/8]
"The Eiffel Tower is located in the city of"

436 words are effectively still in play at that moment. GPT-2 answers " Paris".

Every one of its 50,257 tokens is drawn, once per layer. No subset, no top-k. 603,084 word-scores move per word spoken.

[3/8]
A point lights when that layer pushed the model toward saying it. Violet when it pushed away.

Not a logit lens. An exact decomposition: the residual stream is a running sum, the output head is linear, so the parts sum to the real logits.

Max abs error: 0.00e+0

[4/8]
Tokens are placed by a spectral embedding of GPT-2's own nearest-neighbour graph.

" Monday" → Tuesday Thursday Wednesday Friday
" seven" → six eight five nine
" Paris" → London Copenhagen Brussels Prague

So the map has real regions.

[5/8]
The thing I got wrong, and only found by measuring:

I'd claimed nearby points meant related words. The random 768→3 projection kept 0.3% of the variance, distance correlation r=0.11.

Noise. The claim was false.

[6/8]
Worse — I'd added a radial warp because it looked better. It made fidelity *worse*, 0.11 → 0.08.

Both gone. The layout is 4.6× better than chance now, and the app prints that number at you instead of implying more.

[7/8]
The links are the honest part. Whether two tokens are neighbours is a fact about the model, true wherever the two ends get drawn.

Depth, colour, brightness: exact.
Left/right placement: approximate, and labelled.

[8/8]
GPT-2 also says 2 + 2 = 3, and I kept that in as a preset.

Showing where a model fails is as true as showing where it works.
