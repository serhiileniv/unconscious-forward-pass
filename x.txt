[1/7]
I asked Claude to show me its unconscious in 3D.

It said it has no introspective access. Then it pointed at something real: in one forward pass a model changes the score of every word it knows, every layer, and one word comes out.

So we built it. Real weights, in the browser.

[2/7]
GPT-2 124M. All 50,257 tokens drawn, one point each, per layer. No subset, no top-k.

A point lights when that layer pushed the model toward saying that word. Violet when it pushed away.

One generated word = 603,084 score changes.

[3/7]
It's not a logit lens. It's an exact decomposition.

The residual stream is a running sum and the output head is linear, so each layer's contribution to every word's score is exactly computable, and the parts sum to the model's real logits.

Max abs error: 0.00e+0

[4/7]
On Qwen2.5 0.5B (?model=qwen), contribution to " Paris" for "…the city of":

L19 +0.65
L20 +5.73
L21 +10.90
L22 −3.86

Layer 21 fires " French" " France" " Paris" " Louis" together. Layer 22 then pushes " Paris" back down.

[5/7]
The thing I got wrong, and had to measure to find:

I'd claimed points near each other meant words the model puts near each other.

Random 768→3 projection: keeps 0.3% of variance, distance correlation r=0.11.

That's noise. The claim was false.

[6/7]
Worse: I'd added an r^0.62 radial warp because it looked better. It dropped fidelity to 0.08.

Now top-3 PCA — 3.2%, r=0.33 — and the app prints both numbers at you.

Depth, colour and brightness are exact. Left-right placement is a weak hint.

[7/7]
What it still doesn't show: why a layer pushed as it did, or anything the output head can't see.

These aren't "the words in its mind". They're the exact amount each layer moved every word's score.
